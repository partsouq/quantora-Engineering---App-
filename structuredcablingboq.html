Structured cabling BOQ: outlets, cable, panels and testing

Count the outlets, multiply by a length — and that is precisely why structured cabling quantities so often come out wrong. Here is the build-up done properly.

Structured cabling quantities look simple — count the outlets, multiply by a cable length — and that simplicity is exactly why the numbers so often come out wrong. This guide works through the build-up properly.

1. Count outlets by type, not in total

A data outlet at a desk, a wall-mounted outlet for an access point, a floor box in an open-plan area and an outlet serving a camera or a door controller are different items with different accessories and different installation costs. Count them separately even where the cable is identical.

Note also how many ports each outlet position carries. A "double data outlet" is two links, two patch panel ports and two patch cords at each end — not one.

2. Work out the horizontal cable properly

Horizontal cable is the largest quantity in the package and the one most often estimated badly. The defensible method is an average run per outlet based on the floor plate, with an allowance for routing:

  • Measure or scale the distance from the furthest and nearest outlets to the floor distributor
  • Take the average of the two as a working figure, or calculate per zone on a large floor
  • Add the vertical drop from ceiling void to outlet height, and from tray to distributor
  • Add a routing allowance — cable follows containment around structure, not diagonally
  • Add a service loop at both ends

Check the 90-metre limit. The permanent link for CAT6 and CAT6A is 90 metres, with a 10-metre allowance for patch cords at both ends. Any outlet whose run exceeds this needs an additional floor distributor — a significant cost that must be identified at estimate stage, not on site.

3. Patch panels, cabinets and the passive items

Panel quantity follows outlet count and port density, with spare capacity. The associated items are frequently under-counted:

ItemBasis
Patch panelsTotal ports ÷ panel density, rounded up, plus spare
Patch cordsTwo per link — one at the outlet, one at the panel
Cable managementTypically one 1U manager per patch panel
CabinetSized from total U including switches, panels, managers and growth
PDU, shelves, earthingPer cabinet

4. Backbone and risers

The backbone linking floor distributors to the main distributor is usually fibre, and its quantity comes from the riser route rather than the floor plan. Count the cores, the terminations at each end, the patch panels and the fibre patch cords — each is a separate line and the terminations in particular are frequently forgotten.

5. Testing and certification

Every link must be tested and certified, and this is a real cost proportional to the link count, not a lump sum. Where the specification calls for manufacturer warranty, the certification requirements and the approved-installer condition both affect price. Fibre links require their own test regime.

6. The items with no symbol

  • Containment: tray, trunking, conduit and floor boxes
  • Fire-stopping at every compartment penetration
  • Labelling of every outlet, cable and panel port to the project convention
  • As-built documentation and test result submission

UAE: telecom operator requirements

In the UAE, structured cabling and telecom infrastructure must comply with Telecommunications Regulatory Authority requirements and the standards of the serving operator, du or Etisalat. An operator NOC or infrastructure approval is normally required, and handover typically depends on submitted Fluke certification for copper and fibre links.

That constrains both the specification and the installer, and it belongs in your BOQ preliminaries rather than appearing as a surprise at handover. See our UAE authority compliance guide.
